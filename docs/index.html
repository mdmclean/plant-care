Kalanchoe (Flaming Katy)
📍 Big Room, West-facing window
💧 Last watered 0d ago — next check in ~14d
🌱 Last fertilized 0d ago — next due in ~28d
💡
  • Currently in spectacular peak bloom — massive explosion of bright orange and yellow flowers.
  • Deadheading: As flower clusters fade and brown, snip the flower stems off just above the leaf line with clean scissors. Keeps the plant tidy and encourages remaining buds to open.
  • Reblooming: Once all flowers fade, the plant enters a leafy growth stage. To rebloom next year, give it a 6-week autumn rest period (around September–October) with 14 hours of complete uninterrupted darkness each night and only 10 hours of bright light per day. Combine with very minimal watering. Resume normal care after 6 weeks and buds will form within a few weeks.

💡
  • Teenage Phase: Currently in juvenile phase with solid, healthy, glossy leaves. As the plant grows larger and gets ample bright light, new leaves will naturally develop the famous splits and fenestrations.
  • Support Needed: This is a natural climber — insert a moss pole or coco coir stake into the pot center to give aerial roots something to grip, encouraging larger, more dramatic upright growth.

Money Tree

💧 Last watered 0d ago — next check in ~10d
🌱 Last fertilized 0d ago — next due in ~28d
💡
  • Moving Stress: Money trees dislike being moved frequently. Leaf drop after relocation is normal as it adjusts to new light and humidity. Pick a good spot and leave it be.
  • Trunk Care: The braided trunks are bound when young. As the plant grows, check the top of the braid to ensure nursery ties or wires aren't cutting into the bark and choking the stems.
Anthurium (Flamingo Flower)
💧 Last watered 0d ago — next check in ~7d
🌱 Last fertilized 0d ago — next due in ~28d
💡
  • TLC Needed: Currently showing leggy stems and some dried foliage. Pruning crispy brown leaves at the stem base, moving to a brighter spot, and consistent humidity will help it recover.
  • Grooming: Use clean sharp scissors to snip completely dead or brown leaves right at the base of the stem — keeps the plant tidy and stops it wasting energy on dying foliage.
  • Blooms: The colorful 'petals' are modified leaves called spathes, not true flowers. The actual flowers are the tiny bumps on the central spike (spadix).

Aloe Vera
💧 Last watered 0d ago — next check in ~21d
🌱 Feeding paused — not in active season
💡
  • Drooping lower leaves: Caused by slightly lower light than ideal combined with the weight of water in the leaves. Moving to the sunniest window will help new center growth come in tight and vertical.
  • Maintenance: Clear out dried, crispy brown debris from the base to keep clean and deter pests like mealybugs.
  • First Aid Gel: Clear gel inside mature leaves soothes minor burns. If needed, snip an entire lower leaf from the very base rather than cutting the tip — the plant heals cleanly at the main stem connection.

📍 Dining room, west-facing
💧 Last watered 0d ago — next check in ~14d
🌱 Last fertilized 0d ago — next due in ~28d
💡
  • Currently a beautifully compact, full rosette growing close to the soil. Over time it will naturally drop lower leaves and build a woody trunk, developing a miniature palm-like appearance.
  • Grooming: Wipe long leaves with a damp cloth every few weeks to remove dust and keep them glossy. Gently peel away or snip naturally yellowing lower leaves at the base.

💡
  • Stalled growth: After 3+ years with no new growth in bright indirect light, roots are very likely circling the pot and need more space. Check by sliding the root ball out — tightly wound, circling roots confirm it's time to repot.
  • Propagation: Extremely easy to propagate via leaf cuttings or by dividing pups (offshoots) at the base. Pups can be potted up individually.
  • Resilience: One of the most forgiving houseplants — can tolerate neglect, low light, and irregular watering better than almost anything else.
